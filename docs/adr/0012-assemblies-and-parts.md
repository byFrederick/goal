# ADR 0012 — Assemblies and parts: a plan becomes a parent item whose parts are gated by planned edges

Status: accepted · 2026-10-25 · Builds on the item dependencies of migration 00058 (`item_edges` and the claim gate)

## Context

Some requests need more than one pull request. The station that plans the work (in the software factory, its architect) can split such a request into slices that depend on each other. Until now the split was a list of titles that a person turned into items by hand, and nothing held a slice until what it needed was merged. The owner wants the plan to become work the line runs on its own, like an epic with its stories, and wants it named *assembly* and *parts*, the factory's own words. Everything about it must stay factory config: no station, agent or work type is named in Go, SQL or TypeScript (AGENTS.md rule 1).

## Decision

1. **Names.** The parent is an *assembly* and its children are *parts*, in code, state, config, events, API and docs. The words *effort* and *child* are not used.
2. **The link is `work_items.parent_id`.** A part is an item whose `parent_id` is its assembly and whose `part_key` names it within the plan. `part_paths` holds the files the plan says it changes; it is not called `touches`, which already means a person's touches of an item (migration 00052).
3. **The plan is a platform-defined output field.** The engine will read a `plan` field from any station's output, the same way it reads `lane` (`domain.LaneFrom`) and gate verdicts (`domain.ParseVerdict`), validated in Go: `[{key, title, lane, brief, paths, needs}]`. Which station plans, and its contract, is the factory's definition. The software factory's spec contract and architect guidance are a definition change in the bucket, not in this repository.
4. **One transaction, safe to retry.** `Store.Assemble` moves the planning item to state `assembly` with a fenced transition (its lease recorded as for any transition), creates every part with the parent's type, priority and class of service and its brief as body, and adds one `planned` edge per need, all in one transaction. A plan with no parts, a part without key or title, duplicate keys, an unknown need or a cycle is refused and writes nothing. A retry for an item that already has parts returns them, so a lost acknowledgement never creates duplicates; a unique `(parent_id, part_key)` index backs that.
5. **Parts start at the station that planned them.** Each part is queued at the planning station with its brief, so its own plan or spec is written against main as it is when the part starts, not when the assembly was planned.
6. **Planned edges are the dependency gate.** A part waits for the parts it needs through the existing claim gate: it is not claimed while a need is neither done nor cancelled. An item's waiting reason is derived on read, never stored: `Store.OpenNeeds` reads the open needs of many items of a factory in one query and `domain.WaitingFor` turns them into `after` (a planned or linked need) or `files` (an overlap need). `waiting {kind, items, files}` on `Item` (getItem) and `ItemCard` (the board, item lists and the inbox) shows it, the needs as `<factory>#<number>` refs, absent when the item waits on nothing; until overlap edges are written `files` never appears.
7. **The assembly is never claimed and closes in the store.** State `assembly` is neither queued nor ready and opens no item span. The transition that closes the assembly's last open part also closes the assembly, in the same transaction: done, or cancelled when every part was cancelled. The part's row is locked before the assembly's, every time, so two parts finishing together close it exactly once. While open, an assembly stays at the station that planned it, but it is on no station's column and counts in no station's waiting, in progress, WIP or statistics: the board lists it apart, in `Board.assemblies`, with its parts' summary. Done, it closes at the station of its most recently done part (`LastDonePartStation`), the line's done station, where a finished single item lands, and its `assembly_done` event names that station; cancelled, it keeps its own station, as a cancelled single item does. Migration 00062 moved the done assemblies closed before this at their planning station to their done parts' station.
8. **No nested assemblies.** A part never becomes an assembly; `Assemble` refuses one.
9. **Later steps.** The engine step is built: when a station finishes with a non-empty `plan` (`domain.PlanFrom`) and `board.flow.assemblies.materialize` (default true) is on, the engine calls `Assemble` instead of moving the item on; a malformed plan is a failed output, a rejecting gate ignores the plan, an approving one assembles without merging, and a part's plan blocks the part. Adding a single part to an open assembly is built at Store level: `Store.AddPart` creates it as `Assemble` creates each part, queued at the assembly's station, with its planned edges, `part_created` on the part and `part_added` on the assembly. Its engine operation and API are built: `POST /v1/orgs/{org}/items/{item}/parts` (`addItemPart`, under `item:create`, body `NewPartRequest`: `title` up to 200 characters and `brief` up to 10000, both required, an optional `lane` and `needs`, the `part_key`s of the assembly's parts it waits for) calls `Engine.AddPart`, which trims the input and keys the part by a lowercase slug of its title (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,40}$`, `part` when nothing is left), made unique among the assembly's parts with `-2`, `-3`. It answers 201 with the assembly's `AssemblyParts`, the body `listItemParts` returns, the new part last. Refusals: 400 `part-invalid` (an empty or too long title or brief, an unknown need), 409 `not-an-assembly` (an open item that is not one), 409 `item-closed` (a done or cancelled one), 409 `edge-cycle` and 409 `item-changed` (the assembly closed, or the key was taken, meanwhile); a refusal adds nothing. The dialog on the assembly page is still to come. Cascading cancel is built: `Engine.Cancel` (`POST /v1/orgs/{org}/items/{item}/cancel`, `cancelItem`, under `item:decide`, optional `{reason}`) closes an open item as cancelled with a `cancelled` event and an `item.cancelled` audit entry, taking an in-progress item's lease and stopping its run; a done or cancelled item is 409 `item-closed`. An assembly cancels its open parts in one `Store.CascadeParts` with `Cascade.Cancel` set, each with its own cancelled event, and closes as cancelled even when some part is done; an assembly whose parts are all closed is cancelled with one transition. Still to come: priority and expedite from an assembly to its open parts (pause, resume and cancel cascade already, `Store.CascadeParts`); setting an item's paths from a station's output and holding items whose paths overlap with `overlap` edges at claim time, and keeping their branches fresh; the item page. The API step is built: `ItemState` has `assembly`, `Item` has `part_key` and `part_paths`, and `GET /v1/orgs/{org}/items/{item}/parts` (`listItemParts`) returns an assembly's parts in plan order with done/total, its lead time, and each part's needs, the needs still open (`waiting_on`), its wave (`domain.PartWaves`), pull request, cost and agent. A part's item read (`getItem`) names its assembly in `Item.assembly`, the card's `PartOf` with the part's place in plan order. Every item stores the globs it is expected to change, `work_items.paths` (migration 00060, `domain.Item.Paths`): `Assemble` copies each part's planned paths into it and `Transition.Paths` replaces them; nothing reads them yet. Per-role slots, so one lane's parts cannot fill every slot of a routed station: the definition side is built (a route entry is an agent name or `{agent, agents}`; an entry with `agents: n` keeps n of the station's slots for its lane, which uses no others, entries without a count share the rest, and the counts add up to at most the station's `agents`, checked as `route-agents-exceed-station`; `domain.Station.RouteAgents` carries the counts). Claims that respect each role's slots and board lanes that report them are next. On the board, every card of an item whose run is running says what the agent is doing now (`ItemCard.activity`, from the run's latest tool call) and how far it is through its todo list (`ItemCard.plan_steps`, from its latest TodoWrite call); spec 2.2-E's E7 build note describes them. The edges API is built: `POST /v1/orgs/{org}/items/{item}/needs` (`linkItem`, body `{needs}`) adds a `linked` edge by the caller through `Engine.Link`, and `DELETE /v1/orgs/{org}/items/{item}/needs/{need}` (`unlinkItem`) removes one through `Engine.Unlink`, both under `item:operate` and refused for a done or cancelled item (409 `item-closed`); the other refusals are 400 `edge-invalid`, 409 `edge-cycle`, 409 `edge-exists` and 404 `edge-not-found`. `getItem` lists `needs` and `dependents` (`ItemLink`: the other item's id, ref, current state, and the edge's kind, author and time, oldest first). Still to build on it: `goal link <item> --needs <item>` and `goal unlink` in the CLI, `edge_added`/`edge_removed` and the item's needs and dependents on the web item page, and a note on each open dependent when an item it needs is cancelled. The code-host step of keeping branches fresh is built: `ports.CodeHost.UpdateBranch` updates a pull request's branch with its base at an expected head, never forced, and a conflict is `ports.ErrMergeConflict` (a `*ports.MergeConflictError`), apart from `ErrNotMergeable` (spec 4 §5.5). The engine calls it before every merge (`engine.Merge` and the gate merge), and a conflict sends the item back to be built unless `onConflict` is `human` (spec 4 §4.7). The metrics API reports assembly lead time: `getMetrics` (`Metrics` and its `previous` range, `MetricsSummary`) has `assembly_lead_time_hours` `{p50, p85, assemblies}`, the linearly interpolated percentiles of created → last part done (the assembly's `done_at`) of the assemblies done in the range, cancelled ones left out, null when none were (`ports.FlowMetrics`: `AssembliesDone`, `AssemblyLeadTimeP50`, `AssemblyLeadTimeP85`). Showing its p85 on the Floor next to item lead time is the next step. The board's waiting rows show `ItemCard.waiting` with their three icons: a clock for an item that starts after the items it needs merge, two crossing arrows for one that waits for the same files, a check once it is ready with nothing to wait on (`cardState`, `itemStatus`); the Status filter's "Waiting for same files" keeps them.

## Consequences

- An assembly is visible as an item in its own state, with its parts' done/total, and needs no person to close it.
- Until the API adds `assembly` to `ItemState`, nothing may create one in production: the engine step that calls `Assemble` must not ship before the API step.
- A cascade that locks the assembly and then its parts takes the rows in the opposite order to a closing part. It must take them in one order (for example lock the parts first, by id) or retry on deadlock.
- Flow metrics count parts as items; the assembly itself opens no span, so it adds no station time. A done assembly still counts as an item done in throughput and item lead time; its own lead time, created → last part done, is reported apart as `assembly_lead_time_hours` by the metrics API, and the Floor card that shows it is the next step.
